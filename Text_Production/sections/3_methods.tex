\subsubsection{Liquid media preparation for \textit{Methanobrevibacter}}
\label{sec:liquid_media_preparation}

For DSM~1523, chemicals were mixed in MilliQ-\ch{H2O} as listed in \cref{tab:DSM_1523}, and for DSM~119 following \cref{tab:DSM_119}, except \ch{NaHCO3}. The needed trace element solution and solution A for DSM~1523 and 119 were premixed as shown in \cref{tab:trace_element_sol} and \cref{tab:sol_A}. \ch{FeSO4} x 7 \ch{H2O} solution (0.1\% w/v) and fatty acid mixture were premixed as listed in \cref{tab:FeSO4} and \cref{tab:fat_acid_mix}.

\input{tables/02_Data/DSM_1523_Mischtabelle_base.tex}

\input{tables/02_Data/DSM_119_Mischtabelle_base.tex}

After boiling, the mixture was sparged with \ch{N2} or \ch{N2}/\ch{CO2}. Once the solution was cool enough, \ch{NaHCO3} was added, and the solution was aliquoted into serum bottles, typically 20~ml for routine cultivation. The bottles were also gassed and then sealed with butyl stoppers and aluminum crimp seals. The gas was exchanged to \ch{H2}/\ch{CO2} by evacuating four to six times down to -1~bar and refilling with the gas to 1~bar. Autoclaving (121\,°C for 20~min) was then performed, after which the solution should no longer show any red discoloration.

Before inoculating with 2~ml of a pre-grown \acrlong{mbrevi} culture per 20~ml of medium, 0.2~ml of a sterile \acrshort{dtt} solution (30~g/l; 194.55 mM), sterile solution B (mixed as listed in \cref{tab:sol_B}), and 0.4~ml of a sterile \acrshort{coM} solution (25~g/l) were added. For solution B, the premixed seven-vitamin solution (\cref{tab:seven_vit_sol}) was sterile-filtered and added after \ch{CaCl2} was mixed, aliquoted into a serum bottle, gassed with \ch{N2} or \ch{N2}/\ch{CO2}, and autoclaved. In addition, when needed, antibiotics were added. Furthermore, for DSM~119 Wolin's vitamin solution, prepared according to DSMZ specifications, was sterile added instead of the seven-vitamin solution.
The cultures were then incubated on a shaker at 37\,°C, 120–140 rpm.

\subsubsection{Solid media preparation for \textit{Methanobrevibacter}}

\textbf{Spread \gls{plateing}}

\noindent The media preparation generally followed the instructions for DSM~1523 (\cref{sec:liquid_media_preparation}). However, here 50~mL were aliquoted into a serum bottle containing 0.75~g agar (1.5\%). Another deviation is that here the headspace was not exchanged to \ch{H2}/\ch{CO2}, but instead evacuated and refilled four to six times with \ch{N2} or \ch{N2}/\ch{CO2}. Before pouring the plates, they were heated in a water bath and transferred into the \gls{glove}, where 0.5~mL \acrshort{dtt} (30~g/l; 194.55 mM) and solution B, as well as 1~mL \acrshort{coM} (25~g/l), were added. 100~µl of a pre-grown culture then were spread plated. The plates were then placed into a jar, which was sealed before being taken out of the \gls{glove}. After changing the atmosphere in the jar to \ch{H2}/\ch{CO2}, the plates in the jar were incubated at 37\,°C. If the pressure dropped below 0.7~bar, the gas was refilled.\\

\textbf{Pour \gls{plateing}}

\noindent Here, 50~ml DSM~119 was aliquoted into a serum bottle containing 0-5~g agar (1\%). After transferring the heated DSM~119 (1\% agar) in the \gls{glove} 0.1~ml \gls{dtt}, 0.5~ml solution B and 1~ml \acrshort{coM} was added. After 200~µl of a pre-grown culture were put in the empty petri dish, the still liquid DSM~119 were poured onto it. Following that, the petri dishes were moved in an eight shaped form to distribute the organism. Plates were then transferred outside of the \gls{glove} in a gas tight jar. Before incubated by 37\,°C, the gas was wxchanged to \ch{H2}/\ch{CO2}. If the pressure dropped below 0.7~bar, the gas was refilled.\\

\textbf{Roll plating}

\noindent 10~mL of DSM~1523 is prepared in a serum vial that is usually used for 20~mL of medium, following the procedure described above. Here, the vial contains 0.15~g agar before the prepared medium is aliquoted. Before autoclaving, the gas is exchanged to \ch{H2}/\ch{CO2} by evacuating four to six times to -1~bar. For \gls{plateing}, the serum bottle is heated in a water bath until the agar is liquid, then, after brief cooling, 0.2~mL \acrshort{dtt} and solution B, as well as 0.4~mL \acrshort{coM}, are added. The concentration of these three ingredients is thus double the usual amount. Either 200~µl or 400~µl of a pre-grown culture were added, at which point the medium should still be completely liquid and at a temperature of approximately 50\,°C. Once inoculated, the still completely liquid culture is spun in a horizontal position on an ice bath. It is important that the bottle is rotated as fast as possible in the same horizontal position in the ice bath, so that a clean, thin layer forms around the inside of the serum bottle. Otherwise, the agar becomes chunky, which considerably hinders the identification of growing colonies. Afterwards, the cultures are incubated in a vertical position at 37\,°C, and the gas has to be replenished regularly (every 2 to 4 days).

\subsubsection{Cell counting}
Cell counting was performed with a phase-contrast microscope \textcolor{red}{muss noch ergänzen was für ein Modell und von welcher marke} at 400 x magnification and a Neubauer improved counting chamber, except the \acrshort{woli} sample with an \acrfull{OD} of 0.387 was counted using a Bürker counting chamber. In the Bürker chamber, the squares were counted, whereas in the Neubauer improved chamber, the group squares were counted. Both have a volume of 4~$\cdot$~10\textsuperscript{-6}~mL, as can be seen in \citet{lo-laboroptikgmbhZaehlkammer2026}. It is important to note that counting was not restricted to a single focal plane. Instead, all planes were scanned through and the cells counted accordingly. In addition, the top and left edges of the squares were included in the count, but the bottom and right edges were not. Before cell counting, the \acrshort{OD} was measured with a 20 Genesys\textsuperscript{TM} photometer (Spectronic Instruments, USA). Samples were diluted 10- or 20-fold in DSM~1523. Four chambers were filled for counting, and for each chamber, four squares were counted, except for \acrshort{milli}, where five squares were counted for three chamber fillings, and only two squares for the remaining chamber filling. 

\subsubsection{Sensitivity experiments}

\Acrlong{puro} was tested at five different concentrations (1, 2, 5, 10, and 20~µg/ml) in triplicates using 20~ml DSM~1523 to assess its effect on \acrlong{bovi} and \acrshort{thau} growth. For the three lowest concentrations, a sterile stock solution of 228~µg/ml puromycin in MilliQ-\ch{H2O} was prepared; for the two highest concentrations, a stock solution of 2280~µg/ml was used. A control without any additives was included. Cultures were incubated at 37\,°C and 120--140~rpm. The \acrshort{OD} was measured once a day, using DSM~1523 medium as blank, and dilutions, if necessary, were also performed with DSM~1523. 

The effect of \acrlong{azaeight} on \acrshort{bovi}, \acrshort{thau}, and \acrshort{milli} was tested at five different concentrations (100, 250, 500, 750, and 1000~µg/ml, including a positive control without any additives and a solvent control with 1~ml of \acrshort{dmso}. The experiment was performed in triplicates in 20~ml DSM~1523. A 22.8~mg/ml \acrlong{azaeight} stock solution in \acrshort{dmso} was prepared. Cultures were incubated at 37\,°C and 120--140~rpm. The \acrshort{OD} was measured once a day, using DSM~1523 medium as blank, and dilutions, if necessary, were also performed with DSM~1523.
In addition, an uninoculated control was prepared for the \acrshort{azaeight} sensitivity assay of \acrshort{thau} to assess the influence of \acrshort{dmso} on the medium. For this, 20~ml DSM~1523 was supplemented with 1~ml \acrshort{dmso}. This preparation was incubated under the same conditions (37\,°C, 120--140~rpm), and the \acrshort{OD} was measured once a day.

\subsubsection{Plasmid construction}

In-silico design for suicide plasmids used for \textit{hpt}-gene knockouts in \acrshort{thau} and \acrshort{bovi} was performed with SnapGene (Dotmatics, USA) according to the scheme shown in \cref{fig:general_plasmid_map}. All created plasmids are listed in \cref{tab:plasmid_konstrukte}. For the plasmids, the original \textit{pac} sequence from \textit{Streptomyces alboniger} was codon-optimized for the respective \gls{archa} (\textit{pac\_opt}). All primers used in this study are listed in \cref{tab:primers}. All restriction digestions were performed following the Optimizing Restriction Endonuclease Reaction protocol from NEB (USA). PCR (Mastercycler nexus gradient with flexlid, Eppendorf, Germany) of inserts was performed with Q5\textsuperscript{\textregistered} High-Fidelity DNA Polymerase following the instructions from NEB. Digested vector and PCR DNA fragments were purified on a 1.5\% agarose gel (100~V, 35~min) and extracted using the Monarch Spin DNA Gel Extraction Kit following the manufacturer's instructions, excluding the elution step. Here, a dry centrifugation step (Mikro 22 centrifuge, Andreas Hettich GmbH \& Co. KG, Germany) was added before elution was performed with autoclaved dd\ch{H2O}. In addition, a Biometra TS1 Thermoshaker (Analytik Jena, Germany) was used for heating. All plasmids were assembled via Gibson assembly following the NEB\textsuperscript{\textregistered} HiFi DNA Assembly Reaction protocol. Assembled plasmids were used to transform chemically competent \acrshort{ecolDH} cells and were harvested using the Monarch Spin Plasmid Miniprep Kit, following the kit instructions with two modifications. First, for the lysis step, after addition of lysis solution B2, the mixture was inverted 4 to 5 times, and the neutralization solution was then added directly. The mixture was then inverted until the solution turned completely yellow. Second, elution was not performed with the prescribed elution buffer but with autoclaved dd\ch{H2O}, and a dry centrifugation step was performed once before elution. All plasmids were verified via Nanopore sequencing (Eurofins Genomics GmbH, Luxembourg).

After purifying the DNA fragments and measuring their concentration with the EzDrop 1000 (Blue-Ray Biotech, Taiwan), the pMr\_0321\_hpt backbone and the amplified homology arms were assembled into pMt\_0321\_hpt. 

pMt\_0321\_hpt served as the precursor for the target plasmids (\cref{fig:plasmid_produced}) and was digested with FseI and SmaI to remove the \textit{pac} gene together with its promoter. For the assembly, an insert containing the codon-optimized \textit{pac} gene for the corresponding \acrlong{mbrevi} and the \textit{PhmtB} promoter, with an additional SpeI restriction site between promoter and gene, was designed in-silico with SnapGene and synthesized by IDT. This insert was amplified with primer set 17/63 for \acrshort{thau} and 17/71 for \acrshort{bovi}.
Gibson assembly yielded two plasmids: the correct pMt\_0231\_hpt and pMt\_0231\_hpt containing the codon-optimized \textit{pac} gene for \acrshort{bovi}. The latter was subsequently digested with NotI and AscI, and the \acrshort{bovi} homology arms were amplified with primer sets 52/53 (left arm) and 54/55 (right arm), using extracted \acrshort{bovi} gDNA as template. After purification, the backbone and homology arm regions were assembled to pMb\_0231\_hpt and cloned into \acrshort{ecolDH}.

\subsubsection{Transformation of \textit{Methanobrevibacter}}


All 28 preparation lines are listed in \cref{tab:conjugation_preparation}. \cref{fig:conjugation_method_approach} visualizes the general approach taken in this bachelor thesis to transform three \acrlong{mbrevi} species: \acrshort{thau}, \acrshort{bovi}, and \acrshort{milli}.
Thereby, \acrshort{dap}-\gls{auxo} \acrshort{ecol26} and \acrshort{ecol29} were transformed with plasmids (pMt\_0231\_hpt, pMm\_0231\_hpt, pMb\_0231\_hpt) on selective agar plates containing 0.1~mM~\acrshort{dap} and 30~µg/ml~\acrshort{cap}. 

The day before the mating (Day 0, \cref{fig:conjugation_method_approach}), 5~ml LB medium containing 0.1~mM~\acrshort{dap} and 30~µg/ml~\acrshort{cap} was inoculated with each possible combination of \acrshort{ecol26} and \acrshort{ecol29} and the named plasmids, and incubated overnight at 37\,°C and 120--140~rpm.

On the day of the mating (Day 1), 20~ml LB medium with 0.1~mM~\acrshort{dap} and 30~µg/ml~\acrshort{cap} was inoculated with 1~ml of the overnight \acrshort{ecol} culture and cultivated (37\,°C, 120--140~rpm) for 4.5~h, except for the preparation with \acrshort{bovi}* (\cref{tab:conjugation_preparation}), where 1.5~ml was used for inoculation and the \acrshort{ecol} cultures were cultivated for 3.75~h. Final \acrshort{OD} was measured before the 20~ml \acrshort{ecol} cultures were divided into 10~ml aliquots and centrifuged twice (Centrifuge 5910R, Eppendorf, Germany); the supernatant was decanted, and the pellets were washed once with 1~ml of DSM~1523. Centrifugation was performed for 5~min each time, at 6000~rcf the first time and 9000~rcf the second time.
In addition, 50~ml DSM~1523 (1.5\% agar) was prepared with 10~mM~\ch{MgSO4}, 5~mM~\ch{CaCl2}, and 0.1~mM~\acrshort{dap}. \acrshort{ecol} pellet, the prepared liquid agar medium, as well as a full-grown \acrlong{mbrevi} culture were transferred inside the \gls{glove}. Colonies that had grown on that same day were marked with an asterisk.

Inside the glove box, \acrlong{mbrevi} culture was transferred into 50~µl falcon tubes, centrifuged (10~min, 8000~rcf) outside the glove box, and transferred back into the glove box. The \acrlong{mbrevi} pellet was resuspended in 600~µl DSM~1523. Aliquots of 300~µl of this resuspended culture were used to separately resuspend the \acrshort{ecol26} and WM6029 pellets carrying the corresponding plasmid. These 300~µl mixtures of \acrlong{mbrevi} and \acrshort{ecol} were spot-plated (4 to 5 spots per plate) on the previously described DSM~1523 (1.5\%~agar). The plates were transferred in an airtight anaerobic jar out of the glove box, the gas was changed to \ch{H2}/\ch{CO2}, and the jar was incubated overnight at 37\,°C.

On the following day (Day 2), the incubated plates and two bottles per plate containing 10~ml DSM~1523 medium supplemented with 200~µg/ml \acrlong{cycse} were transferred into the \gls{glove}. The spots were gently scraped off the plate with a spatula together with 2~ml of medium, and 1~ml of this suspension was then transferred back into each of the 10~ml bottles. The bottles were transferred back outside and incubated at 37\,°C and 120--140~rpm for different recovery time periods (t\textsubscript{P}) before adding 20~µg/ml \acrlong{puro}. The 10~ml bottle per plate with the shorter recovery time was marked A (approximately 5~h), and the other B (approximately 20~h). \cref{fig:conjugation_method_approach} visualizes the approach up to this point.

All cultures were subsequently subcultured two more times into fresh selective DSM~1523 medium containing 200~µg/ml \acrlong{cycse} and 20~µg/ml \acrlong{puro}. The time between these two transfers (t\textsubscript{2\_t}) as well as the time from the end of recovery to the first transfer (t\textsubscript{1\_t}) were recorded. The \acrshort{gDna} of growing cultures was extracted using the NucleoSpin\textsuperscript{\textregistered} Microbial DNA Kit following the manufacturer's instructions, except for the elution step, where autoclaved dd\ch{H2O} was used instead of the kit's elution buffer, and a dry centrifugation step was added before elution. For cell lysis, a bead beater (MP Biomedicals) was used. Using the gDNA as template, the 16S rDNA of \gls{archa} was amplified with primer set 22/23 (\cref{tab:primers}), and the bacterial 16S rDNA with primer set 1/2; a PCR with OneTaq\textsuperscript{\textregistered} DNA Polymerase was performed following the instructions from NEB (USA). \gls{S-sequenz} was performed via Sanger sequencing (Eurofins Genomics GmbH, Luxembourg). In addition, a Q5 polymerase PCR was performed with primer set 38/39 to amplify an 838~bp fragment of the suicide plasmids used. Primer set 38/39 was checked for off-target products smaller than 4000~bp in \acrshort{bovi}, \acrshort{milli}, \acrshort{ecol}, and \acrlong{sepidermidis} via NCBI Primer-BLAST (USA), using default settings for primer specificity stringency and the RefSeq reference genomes as the database.

A gel (1.5\%) image was taken of this 838~bp PCR product and of two independently performed 16S rDNA amplifications using different, older primer preparations, which had previously been verified using a negative control. GelRed, Gel Loading Dye Purple (6X), and 1~kb GeneRuler were used as the ladder for the 1.5\% agarose gel.

\subsubsection{Processing CRT-Data} 
\label{sec:processing_CRT_DATA}

\textbf{Cattle enteric fermentation}

\noindent For \acrlong{deu} and \acrlong{nzl} all given data for cattle and its subcattegories "dairy cattle" and "non-dairy cattle" were imported from the "Table3.A" sheet of th CRT tables for each year, and processed so, that for each year of each country a table was created with one column with the year, one column with the cattle type and the following columns are for the measured or calculated values. Afterwards, all one year tables of one country were merged, so that one country has one table, where the values for the cattle categories from 1990 to the last published year are present. 

For the U.S. there are no direct "Dairy cattle" and "Non-dairy cattle" categories, but more detailed one. So after the import of the same value columns for each cattle category as described before, these more detailed subcategories have to be merged in the same 2 as for \acrlong{deu} and \acrlong{nzl}. For this purpose, the official definition and classifications from the U.S. \citep[Chapter 5, p.\,6]{epaInventoryUSGreenhouse2024} were used. \acrshort{DE}, \acrshort{GE} and \acrshort{Ym} were merged as a weighted mean with the populations as weight. The methane emission were summed.\\

\textbf{Methane emission for each sector}

\noindent For sector-specific emissions, only the “TableS3” sheet was used for the most recent CRT (for the U.S., the year 2022; for Germany and New Zealand, the year 2024). Here, all sectors and their subcategories were imported for all years, as were the total emissions. After processing the data, only the methane emission values for the sectors in general were retained, as well as the total methane emissions for each country.

\subsubsection{Calculation of methane emissions from enteric fermentation processes in cattle}
\label{sec:methane_emis_calc_method}

The total methane emission for cattle and its subcategories dairy and non-dairy was calculated following the Tier 2 approach from \textcite{IPCC_2006_complete_Guidline} via the indicators \acrshort{Ym}, \acrshort{GE} and the population number of the animal categories. Therefore, the \ch{CH4} emissions were calculated for dairy and non-dairy cattle following the \cref{eq:emission_total}. The methane emission for cattle than is the sum of dairy and non-dairy.


\begin{equation}
CH_{4,T} = \frac{GE\cdot (\frac{Ym}{100})\cdot 365}{55.65}\cdot \frac{N_T}{10^6}
	\label{eq:emission_total}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $CH_{4, T}$ = yearly methane emission of a animal category [kt CH$_4$ year$^{-1}$]
	\item $GE$ = gross energy intake [MJ head$^{-1}$ day$^{-1}$]
	\item $Ym$ = methane conversion factor [\%]
	\item $55.65$ = energy content of \ch{CH4} [MJ kg$^{-1}$]
	\item $N_T$ = population-size of animal category
	\item $10^6$ = conversion-factor kg to kt
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Calculation of saved methane}
\label{sec:methods-calc_saved_methane}

To calculate the potential methane savings resulting from the mitigation strategy under investigation (immunization of cattle), various success scenarios are defined: 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50\% shifts in the rumen microbiome of cattle from methanogens toward non-methane-producing microbes.
These will have an impact on two indicators used in \citet{IPCC_2019_Chapter_EnterFE}. \acrshort{Ym}, which represents the proportion of energy or feed consumed by cattle that is converted into methane by that microbiome, and \acrshort{DE}, assuming that the reduced amount of feed energy converted into methane now benefits the animal during digestion, meaning that the percentage of feed digestibility for the animal increases.
The values of the resulting Ym (Ym\textsubscript{Red}) and DE (DE\textsubscript{Red}) are calculated as in \cref{eq:Ym_red_sze_calc} and \cref{eq:DE_red_sze_calc}. \newpage

\begin{equation}
	Ym_{Red} = Ym \cdot \left(1 - \frac{Red}{100} \right)
	\label{eq:Ym_red_sze_calc}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $Ym_{Red}$ = adjusted Ym for the investigated reduction scenario [\%]
	\item $Ym$ = original methane conversion factor from CRT [\%]
	\item $Red$ = investigated reduction scenario [\%]
\end{itemize}

\begin{equation}
	DE_{Red} = DE + \left( Ym \cdot \frac{Red}{100}\right)
	\label{eq:DE_red_sze_calc}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $DE_{Red}$ = adjusted DE for the investigated reduction scenario [\%]
	\item $DE$ = Digestibility of feed as a fraction of gross energy intake [\%] 
	\item $Red$ = investigated reduction scenario [\%]
\end{itemize}

\noindent The \cref{eq:emission_total} presents the linear relation of Ym and methane emission of cattle already. DE, on the other hand, is named in three different equations in the calculation of \citet{IPCC_2019_Chapter_EnterFE}, shown here with little adjustments in \cref{eq:GE_berechnung} through (\ref{eq:REG_calculation}). It has his impact on produced methane through his role as value for the calculation of GE, which has a linear relation towards methane emission (see \cref{eq:emission_total}).

\begin{equation}
GE = \frac{\left( \frac{NE_x}{REM}\right) +\left( \frac{NE_y}{REG}\right)}{\left( \frac{DE}{100}\right) }
\label{eq:GE_berechnung}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $GE$ = gross energy intake [MJ head$^{-1}$ day$^{-1}$]
	\item $NE_x$ = sum of the net energy required for maintenance ($NE_m$), for the activity ($NE_a$), for lactation ($NE_l$), for the work ($NE_work$) and for the pregnancy ($NE_p$) of the animal [MJ head$^{-1}$ day$^{-1}$]
	\item $REM$ = ratio of net energy available in a diet for maintenance (\Cref{eq:REM_calculation})
	\item $NE_y$ = sum of the net energy required for growth and for wool production [MJ head$^{-1}$ day$^{-1}$]
	\item $REG$ = ratio of net energy available for growth in a diet (\Cref{eq:REG_calculation})
	\item $DE$ = Digestibility of feed as a fraction of gross energy intake [\%] 
\end{itemize}

\begin{equation}
REM = \left[1.123 - (4.092 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot DE) + (1.126\cdot 10^{-5} \cdot DE^{2}) - \left( \frac{25.4}{DE}\right) \right]
\label{eq:REM_calculation}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $REM$ = ratio of net energy available in a diet for maintenance
	\item $DE$ = Digestibility of feed as a fraction of gross energy intake [\%] 
\end{itemize}

\begin{equation}
	REM = \left[1.164 - (5.16 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot DE) + (1.308\cdot 10^{-5} \cdot DE^{2}) - \left( \frac{37.4}{DE}\right) \right]
	\label{eq:REG_calculation}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $REG$ = ratio of net energy available for growth in a diet
	\item $DE$ = Digestibility of feed as a fraction of gross energy intake [\%] 
\end{itemize}

\noindent To calculate the new methane emission for cattle corresponding to the reduction scenario, GE has to be recalculated with the adjusted DE. Afterwards \cref{eq:emission_total} can be used again to calculate the adjusted \ch{CH4} emission with the adjusted GE (GE\textsubscript{Red}) and Ym\textsubscript{Red}. 
For calculation the GE\textsubscript{Red}, DE\textsubscript{Red} can not just simply be put in the \cref{eq:GE_berechnung}, because of the missing values for NE\textsubscript{x} and NE\textsubscript{y}. Although the values for the individual net energy intake categories (see \cref{eq:GE_berechnung}) should not change, these are not given in the published CRTs of the countries. 
To solve this problem, two things are assumed:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item The net energy required for wool production is an irrelevant category for the investigation of cattle.
	\item Most of the animal in the population of dairy and non-dairy cattle are fully grown or close to it. Because of that the net energy needed for growth will overlooked and set to 0. 
\end{enumerate}

\noindent So, \cref{eq:GE_berechnung} can be slimmed down and rearranged to calculate a fix value for NE\textsubscript{x} for the corresponding year, because the part with NE\textsubscript{y} turns zero (see \cref{eq:NE_x_calc}). For this purpose, GE\textsubscript{Red} is calculated for a given year using DE\textsubscript{Red} and the fixed values for NE\textsubscript{x}, all due to the assumption that NE\textsubscript{y} is 0 (\cref{eq:GE_adjusted_calc}). The calculated gross energy intake is then substituted into \cref{eq:emission_total} along with the corresponding Ym\textsubscript{Red} value. This yields the methane emissions (\ch{CH4}\textsubscript{,R}) for a cattle category under a reduction scenario for a specific year.\newpage

\begin{equation}
	NE_x = GE \cdot REM \cdot \left( \frac{DE}{100}\right)
	\label{eq:NE_x_calc} 
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
	GE = \frac{\left( \frac{NE_x}{REM}\right) }{\left( \frac{DE}{100}\right)}
	\label{eq:GE_adjusted_calc}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $NE_x$ = sum of the net energy required for maintenance ($NE_m$), for the activity ($NE_a$), for lactation ($NE_l$), for the work ($NE_work$) and for the pregnancy ($NE_p$) of the animal [MJ head$^{-1}$ day$^{-1}$]
	\item $GE$ = gross energy intake [MJ head$^{-1}$ day$^{-1}$] (\cref{eq:GE_berechnung})
	\item $REM$ = ratio of net energy available in a diet for maintenance (\Cref{eq:REM_calculation})
	\item $DE$ = Digestibility of feed as a fraction of gross energy intake [\%] 
\end{itemize}

\noindent The saved methane emissions (\ch{CH4}\textsubscript{,S}) for a specific reduction scenario are calculated by subtracting the original value for methane emissions from the value adjusted for the scenario (\cref{eq:saved_CH4})

\begin{equation}
	CH_{4,S} = CH_{4,T} - CH_{4,R}
	\label{eq:saved_CH4}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $CH_{4, S}$ = saved \ch{CH4} through reduction scenario [kt CH$_4$ year$^{-1}$]
	\item $CH_{4, T}$ = yearly methane emission of a animal category [kt CH$_4$ year$^{-1}$]
	\item $CH_{4, R}$ = yearly methane emission of a animal category for a corresponding reduction scenario [kt CH$_4$ year$^{-1}$]
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Prediction of \ch{CH4} emission until 2030}
\label{sec:Prediction_CH4_2030}

Two steps are required to calculate total methane emissions, as well as methane emissions for three reduction scenarios (10, 30, and 50) through the year 2030.
First, total methane emissions without emissions from \acrshort{enterfe} processes in cattle are calculated and then extrapolated.

Second, to calculate the methane emission from \acrfull{enterfe} in cattle, the values for DE, Ym, Population size and NE\textsubscript{x} were predicted for each subcategorie ("dairy" and "non-dairy cattle") until 2030. For that, NE\textsubscript{x} had to be first calculated (\cref{eq:NE_x_calc}). From the predicted values for these indicators the cattle \acrshort{enterfe} \ch{CH4} emission could be calculated (via \cref{eq:GE_adjusted_calc} and (\ref{eq:emission_total})).
Also the the methane emission values for three different reduction scenarios (10, 30 and 50) were calculated with these values using the \cref{eq:Ym_red_sze_calc}, (\ref{eq:DE_red_sze_calc}), (\ref{eq:GE_adjusted_calc}) and (\ref{eq:emission_total}).
Afterwards, the emissions of the subcategories were summed to the \ch{CH4} emission for cattle resulting from enteric fermentation processes.

Third, the predicted emission values for total without cattle enteric fermentation were added with the calculated emission values for cattle enteric fermentation to achieve the total methane emission of a country.

The forecast until was made using a linear regression for each relevant value. However, the regression was not based on the entire time period, but rather on three different time intervals (the last 5, 10, and 15 years). For each linear model, both the \acrfull{ci} with a confidence level of 95\% and the total emissions were calculated. In addition, the R\textsuperscript{2} value was determined for each model.
The extrapolation extends from 2025 to 2030 for \acrlong{nzl} and \acrlong{deu}, and from 2023 to 2030 for \acrshort{usa}. All models with their corresponding equation and R\textsuperscript{2} for each country are listed in \cref{tab:modell_equat_r2_DEU} through \cref{tab:modell_equat_r2_USA}.
