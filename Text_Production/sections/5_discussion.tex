\subsection{Biochemistry}

\subsubsection{Transformation via conjugation}
Promising initial results have been achieved for implementing for the first time a genetic tool for \acrshort{bovi} and \acrshort{milli}, even though a final assessment is still pending due to the pending whole-genome sequencing(see \cref{sec:transformation_via_conjugation}). \\

So in 3 of 28 preparations lines growth could be detected after selection with 20~µg/ml \acrfull{puro} (\cref{tab:conjugation_preparation}). Within these three preparation lines, six cultures exhibited uniform growth of the corresponding \acrlong{mbrevi} species (verified via 16S-sequencing)three each for \acrshort{bovi} and \acrshort{milli}, although the three cultures for\acrshort{bovi} were all from a single preparation line. \\

For these cultures, at least some evidence was provided that they had successfully taken up the plasmid, even though whole-genome sequencing is still pending. The hint is based on a performed \gls{pcr} with the isolated \acrshort{gDna}, where a 838~bp region of the two used plasmids (pMb\_0231\_hpt and pMm\_0231\_hpt, see \cref{fig:plasmid_produced}) was amplified. The gel image of this\gls{pcr} shows a clear band at 750~bp in all six cultures (\cref{fig:gelimage_conj_plasmid_check_epiderm}). Although the expected size for the amplified plasmid region is 838~bp, it is assumed that the 750~bp band is the targeted region, because the used primers were checked for off targets in the gDNA of the possible organisms in the culture. The check with "Primer Blast" on NCBI did not show any possible hits with an PCR product below 1000~bp. Of course, because no negative control was performed, it could be due to unrealistic contamination of the primer set with the plasmid. 

Besides this contamination, the signal in the gel image could be explained to two other possibilities. On the one hand, \acrshort{ecol} could still be present in the culture, and replicate the plasmid, which has the ori for \acrshort{ecol} on it. However, the bacterial \gls{S-sequenz} did not detect a signal for \acrshort{ecol}.

On the other hand, the plasmid could still be present in the culture medium and be extracted along with the gDNA during isolation. What argues against this, however, is that the band at 750~bp exhibits a visually very similar intensity for the three \acrshort{bovi} cultures (see lanes 2, 5, and 6 under “Plasmid” in \cref{fig:gelimage_conj_plasmid_check_epiderm}), although these lanes represent different dilutions or subculturing stages relative to one another; thus, for lane 5 (as the second transfer), only 1\% of the recovery culture from lane 6 should remain in the culture.

For the three \acrshort{milli} lanes (see 1, 3, and 4 in \cref{fig:gelimage_conj_plasmid_check_epiderm}) is it the same, although compared to the \acrshort{bovi} lanes the 750~bp has a lower intensity. In addition, all three \acrshort{milli} show a weak band at just under 500~bp.\\

Derived of the compare for these cultures, some more or less influencing factors for the \gls{conjug} can be assumed, although here a rather small sample was investigated and to verify this further investigation is definitely needed.

First, growth status of \acrlong{mbrevi} seems to be relevant. So all cultures with growth are in the 16 preparation lines with freshly grown cultures (first visible sign of growth on the day the \gls{conjug} was performed). This aligns with the literature, where cultures from late exponential or early stationary phase were used for successful transformation \parencite{capovillaTransformationPlasmidDNA2025, finkShuttlevectorSystemAllows2021}. In which actual growth phase the used initial cultures were is not dectable here, because of missing \acrshort{OD} values for the \acrshort{mbrevi} cultures. This should be changed in future investigation.

Second, all three growing preparation lines had as donator strain for the interdomain \gls{conjug} \acrshort{ecol26}. Future transformation of \acrshort{bovi} and \acrshort{milli} should focus on that. The methylation pattern of \acrshort{ecol26} therefor does not impact the \acrshort{bovi} and \acrshort{milli} \parencite[SI]{schwalenBioinformaticExpansionDiscovery2018a}. 

Third, the recovery time is not so critical for the success (growing preparation line no. 6.6A had a recovery time of 4.5~h while 5.6B of 19.5~h, see \cref{tab:conjugation_preparation}) of the transformation as described for \textit{Methanothermobacter thermautotrophicus} $\delta$H in \citet{finkShuttlevectorSystemAllows2021}.

Fourth, for the \acrshort{OD} of the \acrshort{ecol26} no direct trend is detectable from this preparation set, though the range of the \acrshort{OD} was 1.24 to 1.79  that seems to work.\\

A problem is, that also leads to the missing whole genome sequncing, \gls{pcr} of the bacterial 16S-\acrshort{rRNA} produces clearly a signal at 1000~bp (see \cref{fig:gelimage_conj_plasmid_check_epiderm}), which was sequenced as derived from \acrshort{sepidermidis}. Because of that, the next step would be to purify these cultures through plating on selective media and picking a single colonie for the following analyses.\\

If whole-genome sequencing confirms that the plasmid has been successfully integrated, the next step would be to test the counter-selection implanted in the plasmid design using \acrlong{azaeight}.  Also, it would be interesting to test if other transformation methods as electroporation could work.

To improve the \gls{conjug} protocol, it would likely be useful to first test whether a specific growth phase of \acrshort{mbrevi} has an effect by measuring its \acrshort{OD} beforehand, because a clear trend towards fresh grown cultures is visible for this \gls{conjug} experiment. 

To determine whether the \acrshort{OD} of \acrshort{ecol26} influences success—or whether the ratio of \acrshort{ecol} cells to \gls{archa} cells does, as described, for example, as a critical factor in \textcite{koppCriticalVariationsConjugational2004} for the \gls{conjug} of \textit {Sorangium cellulosum}, further test series must be conducted.

\label{sec:plasmid_construction_diskussion}

The most interesting finding for the plasmid construction, is the discovery of \acrlong{aa} sequence of the \textit{pac} gene, which was on the parent plasmid and originally planned to be on the new constructed ones for the \gls{conjug} and is also several times used in the literature \textcolor{red}{Max du hattest das in der literatur doch auch gennant gesehen oder?}. So it differs in 5 aminoacids from the \acrlong{aa} sequence in the originally \textit{pac} from \textit{Streptomyces alboniger} that leed to an longer \gls{betaFaltblatt} at the \gls{cterm} site (see \cref{fig:pac_org_and_old}).\\

If the changed 3-D-structure really has an influence on the function or the performed conjugation is not clear. To answer this question it needs further investigation.

In general the constructed plasmids followed the scheme in \cref{fig:general_plasmid_map}, where the selection of a successful conjugation works with a resistance against puromycin via \textit{pac}-gene and a counterselection with a sensitivity against \acrfull{azaeight}. 

If the whole-genome sequencing proofs the success of the transformation, a next step for the plasmid experiments would to test the other planed plasmids with a different promoter (\textit{PglnA} instead of \textit{hmtB}, see \cref{tab:plasmid_konstrukte}).

\subsubsection{Sensitivity tests}

\textbf{Puromycin}

Since the selection of successfully transformed \acrlong{mbrevi} is based on resistance to \acrfull{puro}, it was first necessary to determine whether, and at what concentration, \acrshort{bovi} and \acrshort{milli} are sensitive to the antibiotic. \\

The growth curves in \cref{fig:ab_mbovis_puromycin} for \acrshort{bovi} showed that it exhibits no real sensitivity up to a concentration of 2~µg/ml~\acrshort{puro}. At 5~µg/ml, an initial change in the growth curve can be observed. At 10~µg/ml and 20~µg/ml, the organism appears to be more sensitive, although growth could be detected through spontaneous resistance at 10~µg/ml after 4–6 days and at 20~µg/ml after approximately 8 days.

\acrshort{thau} appears to be more sensitive to \acrshort{puro} (see \cref{fig:ab_mthau_puromycin}). Thus, a change in the growth curve can already be observed at a concentration of 2~µg/ml, and at concentrations of 10~µg/ml and 20~µg/ml, there is no growth at all within 10 days.

\textcolor{red}{Should I also mention here that in another series of experiments with millerae conducted by the research group, it was already determined that this strain also shows no growth at 20 µg/mL within 11 days?}\\

Selection using \acrshort{puro} therefore works for \acrshort{bovi} and \acrshort{thau}. For the upcoming conjugation experiments, a concentration of 20~µg/ml~\acrlong{puro} was chosen for selection. Should growth occur within 8 days, it can be assumed that this was mediated by the introduced resistance.\\

\textbf{\acrlong{azaeight}} \textcolor{red}{hier muss ich definitiv noch einkürzen}

As for the second and relevant function of the plasmid in the genetic modification of the \acrlong{mbrevi} genome, namely, the counter-selection with \acrfull{azaeight}, it is interesting to know how sensitive the organism is to the base analog. Although the \textit{hpt} gene on the plasmid already provides a backup mechanism that induces artificial sensitivity to \acrshort{azaeight}. Concentrations of 100, 250, 500, 750, and 1000~µg/ml~\acrshort{azaeight} were tested, hereby the \acrshort{azaeight} dose not dissolve in \ch{H2O}, therefore \acrshort{dmso} was used as solvent.\\

As can be seen from \cref{fig:aza_mbov}, \ref{fig:aza_mmill}, and \ref{fig:aza_mthau}, this series of measurements for \acrshort{bovi}, \acrshort{milli}, and \acrshort{thau} has little to no predictive value regarding sensitivity to \acrlong{azaeight}. However, the solvent control revealed a problem: \acrshort{dmso} itself has an inhibitory effect on the growth of the tested \acrlong{mbrevi} cultures and is therefore not suitable as a solvent for the base analogs in this context.\\

In the case of \acrshort{bovi}, a slight effect of \acrshort{azaeight} can still be observed (see \cref{fig:aza_mbov}). For example, the triplicates for the sample with a final concentration of 100~µg/ml~\acrshort{azaeight} show a curve very similar to that of the DMSO sample, even though only one-tenth of the DMSO volume was added to the 22.8~ml culture (0.1~ml to 1~ml). The maximum measured \acrshort{OD} value, at approximately 0.1, is slightly below that of the positive control (0.2), and growth was delayed by one to two days. At 250~µg/ml, growth was delayed by an additional 2 days, and at concentrations of 500~µg/ml and higher, no \acrshort{OD} values above 0.5 were measured within 13 days, with the exception of one replicate for the 1000~µg/ml solution. This indicates—particularly for the last three concentrations, but also between 100 and 250 µg/ml—that the base analog has an effect on the growth of \acrshort{bovi}; however, the extent of this effect cannot be determined, since the solvent DMSO itself inhibits growth by one-third.

For \acrshort{milli}, DMSO also shows an inhibition of approximately one-third compared to the positive control, even though a delay in growth could not be detected, as the second measurement was not taken until three days later. In addition, the 100~µg/ml preparation again resembles the DMSO control, but no pattern is discernible beyond that. For example, the 1000~µg/ml preparation grows almost exactly like the 100~µg/ml preparation; the 750~µg/ml sample includes one replicate that even reaches maximum \acrshort{OD} values comparable to the positive control, as well as one replicate that showed no growth at all (see \cref{fig:aza_mmill}).

In the case of \acrshort{thau}, even less growth is visible, since—apart from an outlier reading on one day (Day 2) for a replicate of the DMSO sample—growth is only visible in the positive control. It should be noted, however, that growth occurred only after 5 days for this one replicate and after 9 and 14 days for the other two replicates. Furthermore, no \acrshort{OD} value greater than 0.2 was measured.\\

A plausible explanation for the negative effect of DMSO on the growth of \acrshort{mbrevi} species is that, as reported in \cref{atcherEffectDMSOAqueous2013}, DMSO acts as a mild oxidizing agent toward dithiols at room temperature. This would result in the \acrshort{dtt}, which is added to the culture as a reducing agent to remove residual oxygen and keep the medium completely anaerobic, reacting with the DMSO to form its cyclic structure with a disulfide bridge, whereas \acrshort{dmso} reacts to form dimethyl sulfide (\cref{sanzSimpleSelectiveOxidation2002}). Consequently, no reducing agent is present in the medium, and the cultures are exposed to greater oxidative stress.\\


This hypothesis is supported by the following:

First, when samples were collected for photometric measurements, the cultures containing DMSO turned red more quickly and also took longer to lose their color than the positive controls or than had previously been observed in other cultures (\cref{sec:azaeight_result}).

Second, in the \acrshort{thau} measurement series, a DSM 1523 medium was also prepared as a triplicate; this was not inoculated but only had 1~ml~DMSO added. Here, as with the other cultures, the \acrshort{OD} was measured daily. Within a few days, the culture turned distinctly red and did not fade (see \cref{fig:dmso_neg_kontrolle}).


However, this also means that the \acrlong{mbrevi} themselves can partially reduce the solution, since in this case the cultures did not turn red as quickly or as distinctly as in the negative control, and decolorization also occurred repeatedly. However, the \acrlong{mbrevi} do not appear to be able to fully compensate for the absence of \acrshort{dtt}, resulting in inhibited growth.\\

In the future, an alternative solvent should be sought.

\subsubsection{General characterization of \acrlong{mbrevi}}

Besides the experiments leading to the first attempt to transform \acrshort{thau} and \acrshort{bovi}, general characterization experiments were performed to improve our understanding of the \acrlong{mbrevi} species in the rumen of cattle. \\


\textbf{Cellcount per ml}

An important value to know for diffrent experiements is the cellcount per ml for a specific \acrshort{OD} (e.g. for plating to calculate the plating efficiency or to calculate the ratio of \acrshort{ecol} and \acrfull{mbrevi} for the conjugation). For this bachelor thesis first reference values were counted with a cell-chamber and a \gls{pha_con_mic} for single \acrshort{OD} of \acrshort{thau}, \acrshort{bovi}, \acrshort{milli} and \acrshort{woli} (see \cref{tab:cellnumb}).\\

Compared to the cellcount per ml for \acrshort{ecol} ($8 \cdot 10^8$~cells/ml) at a \acrshort{OD} given by \textcite{merckkgaaCellDensityMeasurement2026} the counted cells/ml for the \acrshort{mbrevi} cultures are quite high (e.g. see \cref{tab:cellnumb} \acrshort{woli}: $10.819 \cdot 10^8$~cells/ml at a \acrshort{OD} of 0.387). 

Since only one or two cultures have been counted for these organisms so far, these values apply only to the measured \acrshort{OD} values. Whether a linear relationship exists—as is generally assumed for \acrshort{OD} values $\unlhd$ 0.3 \parencite[9.\,37] {steinbuechelMicrobiologyLabExperiments2021}, remains entirely unclear at this point. Nor can it be conclusively stated here whether there are differences between the \acrshort{mbrevi} species; one indication of this could be that \acrshort{bovi} has more cells/mL with an \acrshort{OD} that is 0.1 smaller.

Conducting another cell count at various \acrshort{OD} values would be helpful in clarifying this and creating a calibration curve.\\

Two factors contributed to the inaccuracy of the count.

First, the chamber depth of 0.1~mm \parencite{lo-laboroptikgmbhZaehlkammer2026} was so great that the cells could be located in multiple planes, requiring the count to switch back and forth between them. Here, cells can easily be overlooked because the correct plane was not selected, or they can be counted twice.

Second, at 400x magnification, the \acrshort{mbrevi} cells are still quite small on a \gls {pha_con_mic}, and they also tend to form chains or clumps, which carry the risk of no longer being able to distinguish cells from one another (compare the example image shown in \cref{fig:cell_count_bovis_bsp}).

To mathematically mitigate the increased risk of miscounting, an attempt was made to count approximately 150 to 250 cells per large square instead of fewer than 150. This, of course, leads to a significantly increased workload.\\

To further address this issue in the future, one could utilize the intrinsic fluorescence of methanogenic archaea due to the cofactor F\textsubscript{420} \parencite{lambrechtFlowCytometricQuantification2017} and perform the counting using fluorescence microscopy. This would make it easier to identify the small cells at 400x magnification. A 1,000x magnification is difficult to achieve during specimen preparation due to the height of the counting chamber and the required distance from it.\\

\textbf{Cultivation} 

As discussed in \cref{sec:intro_biochem} most of the described media for the cultivation of \acrshort{bovi} and \acrshort{thau} contain rumenfluid \parencite{liMethanobrevibacterBoviskoreaniJH1T2023, leeMethanobrevibacterBoviskoreaniSp2013, millerDescriptionMethanobrevibacterGottschalkii2002}. In this bachelor thesis the \acrshort{mbrevi} species could be cultivated successfully in DSM 1523 and 119, which do not contain any rumenfluid. Because there was no visible difference between the cultivated cultures in these two media, most of the experiments were performed in DSM 1523, which is easier to handle due it does not contain the toxic fatty acid mix and the sludge fluid.\\

The positive controls of the sensitivity tests towards \acrlong{puro} (\cref{fig:ab_mbovis_puromycin} and \ref{fig:ab_mthau_puromycin} in \cref{sec:AB_sens_result}) and \acrlong{azaeight} (\cref{fig:aza_mbov}, \ref{fig:aza_mmill} and \ref{fig:aza_mthau} in \cref{sec:azaeight_result}) are indirect growth-curve for \acrshort{thau}, \acrshort{bovi} and \acrshort{milli} in DSM 1523. In general, it can be said, based on these growth-curves, that the used \acrshort{mbrevi} reach their growth maximum within 2 to 4 days. This maximum is usually around a \acrshort{OD} of 0.3 and does not exceed 0.4. For calculating general growth parameter as geration time or growth rate furteher growth experiements are neede with a higher time resolution, espcially in the exponential growth phase. 

Compared with growth of methanogenic \gls{archa} in the literature \parencite{liMethanobrevibacterBoviskoreaniJH1T2023, hoedtDifferencesDownunderAlcoholfueled2016}, are they growing as fast as the reference organism or a bit faster, but in the litrature \acrshort{OD} values of 1.2 could be achieved for some cultures. This difference in growth density could be due to the missing rumen fluid, but needs further investigation.\\

If we look at the cultivation of \acrshort{bovi} and \acrshort{thau} a bit more closely, it can be said for \acrshort{bovi} that it grows quit reliably in the described manner and can also be easily spread plated (see \cref{fig:spread_plating}). The only unusual aspect is the formation of precipitates (see \cref{fig:bovis_präzipitat}), which makes photometric measurements somewhat more difficult. In this case, it is important to homogenize the culture as thoroughly as possible beforehand, even if the precipitates cannot be completely dissolved.

The \acrshort{thau} on the other hand grows much more unreliably, as you can see in the comparism of the positiv control for \acrshort{puro} and \acrshort{azaeight} (\cref{fig:ab_mthau_puromycin} and \cref{fig:aza_mthau}), where the triplicates for \acrshort{azaeight} started to grow after 5 to 14 days while for \acrshort{puro} within 3 days the max. \acrshort{OD} was reached. In addition, this \acrshort{mbrevi} species does not grow as dense as the other in this bacherlor thesis and lyses pretty quick afterwards (e.g. within 4 days all triplicates of the positiv control in \cref{fig:ab_mthau_puromycin} are below 0.1). This growth problem is also showing in plating. Here the spread plating on DSM 1523 (1.5\% agar) and pour plating with DSM 119 (1\% agar) were not successful (\cref{fig:spread_plating} and \ref{fig:pour_plating}). The roll plating was successfull but \acrshort{thau} bottle only 15 colonies while the others had above 2000 (see \cref{tab:roll_plat_eff}). 

This growing behavior of \acrshort{thau} makes it more difficult to analyses it, and also to plan its grows for the transformation, where it is necessary that the culture grows within two or three days.\\ 

\textbf{Roll plating}

The advantage of roll plating over spread or pour plating is that it can be performed in a standard laboratory and does not require an anaerobic atmosphere. Only the picking of the colonies must be done inside a glove box. In general, it can be said that the technique works well, and for \acrshort{bovi} and \acrshort{woli}, less starting material will need to be used next time, since there are too many colonies, which are consequently too small for selective picking (see \cref{fig:roll_platt} and \cref{tab:roll_plat_eff}). An important aspect of the roll-out technique is that the agar must still be completely liquid when the culture is added and must be spun into the same horizontal position as quickly as possible while rotating in the ice bath; otherwise, the agar becomes chunky, as is easily recognizable in the case of \acrshort{woli} (see \cref{fig:roll_platt}), and this significantly hinders the identification of growing colonies. It is also important to regularly replenish the gas mixture of \ch{H2} and \ch{CO2} to enable growth.\\

The values shown here in \cref{tab:roll_plat_eff} are very low, with values in the lower two-digit ppm range—and even below 1 ppm for \acrshort{thau}—but these values should be interpreted with caution, since the \acrshort{OD} of the initial solution was not measured; instead, it was assumed retrospectively that the cultures have a similar OD to those obtained by cell counting.

For future determinations of plating efficiency, it would be important to measure the \acrshort{OD} in advance and to plate only at an \acrshort{OD} corresponding to those in \cref{tab:cellnumb}.
